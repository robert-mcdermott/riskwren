# ADR-0012: Ship only content that may be shipped, and build it from its source

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-11-25

## Context

A compliance product is only as useful as the frameworks it knows. The frameworks organizations are held to come under very different terms. Works of the United States government are in the public domain. International and industry standards are copyrighted, and several forbid redistribution, adaptation, or use inside a tool without a paid license. RiskWren is Apache 2.0 licensed, and an adopter must be able to rely on that for everything in the repository.

Framework content also changes. Catalogs typed in by hand drift from their source and are expensive to revise.

## Decision

- **Every pack declares a text policy**, and a test enforces it ([content-packs.md](../content-packs.md)).
- **Public-domain frameworks are shipped in full**, generated by a committed script from the publisher's own machine-readable file, with the source and its version recorded in the pack.
- **Copyrighted frameworks are shipped, at most, as codes with RiskWren's own labels**, and only where the publisher's terms allow that. Their text is never shipped. A license holder imports the official text locally, and it never leaves that installation.
- **Frameworks whose terms forbid use inside a product are not shipped at all.** RiskWren provides the means to load them, as it does for a grant's terms or a data use agreement.
- **Content written for RiskWren** (the default program, the risk library, the starter controls, questionnaires) is original and Apache 2.0 licensed.
- **The Open Security Controls Assessment Language is the interchange format** for catalogs, baselines, implementations, assessment results, and plans of action, read and written with the standard library.
- **Methodologies are described in RiskWren's own words.** Published risk and audit methodologies that are copyrighted or trademarked are not reproduced or named as product features.

## Consequences

- The repository stays cleanly Apache 2.0, and each framework page in the app shows its publisher and license.
- The built-in list is weighted toward public standards and regulations. Organizations held to licensed standards do a one-time import.
- A new release of a source catalog is a rebuild and a comparison.
- Crosswalks from the starter controls to each framework are RiskWren's own work and must be maintained as frameworks change.

## Alternatives

- **Paraphrasing copyrighted standards:** a derivative work by another name, and a risk for every adopter.
- **Shipping no frameworks:** safest, and it would abandon the promise of working on the first day.
- **A proprietary content subscription:** how commercial products solve this, and incompatible with an open project.
